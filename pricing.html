Case 01 · Rate Change & Routing

Four carriers raised rates.
Where is the money, and what does it cost in service?

August network: 2,000 parcels, 8 destinations, 4 carriers, each with old and new rate cards, fuel surcharges, minimums, remote surcharges and transit times. The job was to size the increase, find savings that do not break the delivery promise, and flag what could go wrong in rollout.

Cost bridge · August network

From old rates to the optimized network

Savings are only counted on shipments that have a valid historical quote and at least one carrier that meets the promised transit time. Moving 407 parcels off incumbents that cannot hit the promise costs money, and the bridge shows it instead of netting it away.

Change the rules, re-route the network

Same logic as the workbook: billable weight is the greater of actual and DIM weight (divisor 139), rounded up to the next pound; each carrier is priced at its new base rate plus fuel, floored at its minimum, plus the remote surcharge, and it only qualifies if its transit time meets the shipment's promise. The workbook did not enforce carrier capacity; the capacity switch here is an extension I added.

32% of parcels arrived late historically, more than the matrix predicts. Before routing on planned transit, validate it against observed lanes.

Recommendation

  1. Pilot SLA-compliant routing on the 1,982 eligible parcels; never trade the promise for rate.
  2. Phase Pacific in. It takes the most new volume; keep Meridian as overflow and confirm capacity in writing.
  3. Weeks 1 to 2: track on-time rate by carrier and lane, billed vs. modeled cost, and Pacific volume against its cap.

How it is built

One LET formula per carrier column prices a shipment and blanks it when the carrier cannot meet the promise. Twelve automated checks reconcile record counts, both cost bridges, and SLA breaches; all pass.

=LET(rate, XLOOKUP($P8, RateCards[Key], RateCards[Meridian New], 0), days, XLOOKUP($C8, Transit[Destination], Transit[Meridian], 0), IF(OR(rate<=0, days=0, days>$I8), "", ROUND(MAX(rate*(1+NewFuel), Minimum) + IF($H8="Y", Remote, 0), 2)))

Case 02 · Rate Card Comparison

Three vendors, three pricing structures.
Which one, and how sure are we?

A take-home exercise: price 450 shipments to five countries under three vendors that bill differently. Vendor 1 uses a weight-bracket table; Vendor 2 charges per piece plus per pound; Vendor 3 per piece plus per kilogram. The billing increment was not specified, so I modeled it both ways and the answer flips.

Total cost on 354 priced shipments

By country

Coverage gap first. 96 shipments (21%) go to Afghanistan, which no vendor quotes. Totals understate true spend until that lane has a carrier, a restricted-lane policy, or turns out to be a data error.
Assumptions on the page. One piece per shipment, Vendor 1 minimum of 1 lb, one common currency, no fuel, remote or DIM charges supplied. Each one is listed in the workbook with what changes if it is wrong.
